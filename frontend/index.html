Physiology

The body announces TPP before it arrives.

Thyroid hormone excess keeps the sympathetic nervous system in overdrive. As an episode approaches, that overdrive intensifies and potassium shifts into cells — changes that surface in heart rate, HRV and the QT interval hours before muscle weakness.

Signal path

From a raw heartbeat to a risk score.

Every number on this page comes from one sensor and one stream. Six stages, each with a single job.

01 — 06

Stage 01

One sensor, one waveform

A single-lead ECG sensor sits on the body and streams the raw electrical signal — P wave, QRS complex, T wave — sampled continuously and sent over Bluetooth or cable. No wearables, no derived step counts: just the heart's own electrical trace.

Stage 02

Remove what the body adds

Raw ECG carries movement noise, 50 Hz mains interference and respiratory drift. A bandpass filter keeps the heart's band, a notch filter removes the power line, and baseline correction flattens the slow swing — leaving a waveform the next stage can trust.

Stage 03

Find every R peak

Pan-Tompkins style detection locates the apex of each QRS complex. The gap between consecutive peaks is the RR interval in milliseconds — the raw material for heart rate and every HRV metric that follows.

Stage 04

Turn intervals into physiology

HR is 60,000 divided by the mean RR interval. From the RR series come SDNN, RMSSD and the LF/HF ratio — a live picture of autonomic balance. From the waveform come QTc, T-wave amplitude and ST behaviour, the electrical signature of a potassium shift.

Stage 05

Read the trend, not the snapshot

Each 15-minute window is compared with your own seven-day baseline over a six-hour lookback. An LSTM learns how those trends unfold in time; XGBoost combines the learned pattern with tabular triggers into a single risk score between 0 and 1.

Personal baseline

Compared with you, not the population.

A resting heart rate of 86 bpm means something different for every patient. NuraSesne keeps a rolling seven-day baseline and scores your deviation from your own normal — then keeps learning as it settles.

Model

A slow signal needs a model with memory.

TPP precursors unfold over hours, not seconds. The LSTM holds that history; XGBoost turns it into a decision you and your clinician can inspect.

Input

6-hour trends

HR, HRV and QTc windows against your baseline

Stage one

LSTM

Learns how the hours before an episode unfold

Stage two

XGBoost

Weighs trends with tabular triggers and time of day

Output

Risk 0–1

Two-hour probability, thresholded with your clinician

Trend, not snapshot

The model reads the slope of heart rate, HRV and QTc — a single odd reading is not enough to raise an alert.

Explainable by design

Every alert can show which features pushed the score up, so you and your clinician can judge whether it makes sense.

Tuned with your clinician

Thresholds are set per patient and reviewed as your personal baseline settles, rather than fixed for everyone.

Scope

An early-warning instrument, not a diagnosis.

01

One lead, one signal

A single-lead ECG sees rhythm and repolarisation, not everything. It is one input into your care, not a full cardiac workup.

02

Probabilities, not promises

A risk score expresses likelihood. It can rise without an episode and can miss one — your clinician's judgement always comes first.

03

Emergency care still applies

If you cannot move, collapse, or have trouble breathing, contact emergency services. Do not wait for an alert.
